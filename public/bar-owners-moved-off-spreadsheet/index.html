Bar owners who moved off the spreadsheet: what did they go with?

It depends which job the spreadsheet was doing. If Sunday or Monday is spent splitting and paying tips, bar owners usually need tip-management or payroll software; if the job is assembling sales, labour and cost figures from till and rota exports, a spreadsheet that refreshes from those exports can replace the retyping — and many bars need both. Carlos Alves Consulting does the second job, inside Excel or Google Sheets you already own; it does not sell tip-management or payroll software.

What were you using the spreadsheet for?

That is the decision. A Sunday export from the till into a workbook can be:

  • Tip math — pooling, sharing and getting amounts ready for payroll.
  • The weekly pack — sales, labour, margins and a view you can trust by Monday morning.
  • Both, which is why buying one product and still retyping the rest is so common.

Moving “off the spreadsheet” only works if the new setup actually covers the job you were doing by hand.

Frequently asked questions

We already export from the till every Sunday. Can a refreshable spreadsheet replace that ritual?
The export step often still exists — the till has to produce a file. What can go is the retyping, the broken copy-and-paste, and the Monday rebuild. Reporting automation connects those exports so the weekly pack updates from the files you already get.
Is a tip-management product enough on its own?
Only if tip calculation and payment was the whole job. If you still paste sales and labour into a weekly workbook after the tips are done, you have a second job. The reverse is also true: an automated weekly pack will not pay the tronc.
Will we need to buy new software or a subscription for the reporting work?
