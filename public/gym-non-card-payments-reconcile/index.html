How can a gym reconcile cash and bank-transfer payments with its gym software without marking each one by hand?

Most gym software can already hold non-card payments: you record the payment as an offline method (cash or bank transfer) against the member, then confirm it, and TeamUp's own help centre says pending offline payments can be confirmed one by one or in bulk. The step that usually stays manual is checking which pending payments have actually arrived, and that matching can be done in a refreshable spreadsheet that lines up your payment export against the gym system's pending list, so staff only confirm what matches and chase what doesn't.

The question, asked in r/gymowner, was:

"I am looking to find a way to accept these payments but then have them automatically (or with a low touch) reconcile with my gym software in terms of marking as paid or providing class credits."

Why do non-card payments end up being marked one by one?

Card and direct-debit payments taken inside gym software are recorded by the software itself. Cash, bank transfers and payment apps happen outside it, so two separate records exist: the money (in a cash book, a bank statement or an app's history) and the member's account (pending, overdue or paid). Somebody has to check one against the other before marking anything as paid. That check, done line by line, is the "one by one" job the question describes.

Verdict: first switch on your gym software's offline payment methods, so every non-card payment exists as a pending item rather than a note. Then, if checking those items against the bank or app is still taking hours, that matching is a spreadsheet job, and the confirmation in the gym software can be done in bulk.

Is there a way to do it with a CSV or Excel file?

Usually, yes, in two halves:

  1. Export both sides. A bank statement or payment-app history as CSV, and a pending or overdue payments list from the gym software. UNVERIFIED: which gym systems let you export their pending offline payments; check with your provider.
  2. Match them in one sheet. A spreadsheet using Power Query (built into Excel) can bring in both files each week and match on amount, date and a member reference, then list what is clearly paid, what is missing and what needs a person to look at it.

The member reference is the part that makes or breaks it. If members pay by bank transfer with their name or membership number as the reference, matching is far more reliable than matching on amount alone.

When does a reconciliation spreadsheet help, and when does it not?

It helps when the payments already land somewhere you can export (a bank account, a payment app or a cash book) and the slow part is checking them against members. It also helps when the same figures then go into a weekly report on income, attendance and staffing.

It does not help if the gym software has no offline payment method at all, because there is then nothing to confirm against. It will not take payments, write back into your gym software or issue class credits. Those stay with your gym software provider.
