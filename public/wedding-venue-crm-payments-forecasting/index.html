Is a venue CRM the same as event-management software?

Not quite. A general CRM tracks contacts and a sales pipeline. A wedding venue also needs quotes or proposals, contracts, deposits and instalments, a diary of viewings, tastings and events, and details such as guest numbers and packages. Products sold as venue or event-management software bundle those together, and that bundle matches most of the list in the question.

Which requirements should a venue check most carefully?

The ones the vendor pages fetched did not settle:

  • Couple portal: a personal area per couple to see bookings, quotes and documents.
  • Booking-system sync: the question names Bookalet and iCal.
  • UK payments: card is common; check bank transfer and how deposits and instalments are handled in the UK.
  • Revenue forecasting: "reports" and "dashboards" are not the same as a forecast of confirmed and likely revenue by month.

When does reporting automation help a venue, and when does it not?

It helps when the venue already has its bookings in a system (or a spreadsheet) and someone still builds the weekly or monthly picture by hand: confirmed events, deposits received, balances due, food and drink costs from invoices, and a view of the months ahead. Do wedding venues need a CRM or event-management software?

If the list includes quotes, contracts, deposits, an events diary and a couple portal, event or venue-management software is the closer fit, because those products bundle that work. A general CRM may cover leads and the pipeline but leave the rest to other tools.

Planning Pod's venue page says it schedules payment instalments and sends automated reminders. Tripleseat's homepage says it replaces payments chased by phone. Check UK payment methods with each vendor.
